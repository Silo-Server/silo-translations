# Latvian (lv)

## Voice

- Address the user politely with the plural **jūs**, as Google and Microsoft do in Latvian:
  *Pierakstieties*, *Atlasiet profilu*, *Mēģiniet vēlreiz.*, *Jūsu bibliotēka*. Write *jūs*,
  *jūsu* in lowercase and leave the pronoun out where the verb already says who. Never the
  informal *tu* (*Atlasi*, *tava*), also in text that speaks to a group.
- Buttons, menu items and the choices of a picker use the infinitive, consistently with the
  strings beside them: *Saglabāt*, *Atcelt*, *Dzēst*, *Pierakstīties*, *Mēģināt vēlreiz*.
  Instructions in sentences use the *jūs* imperative: *Atlasiet bibliotēku*. Names of settings,
  screens and sections are nouns: *Atskaņošana*, *Lejupielādes*, *Straumes kvalitāte*.
- An on/off value is *Ieslēgts* / *Izslēgts*, whatever it switches.
- Progress states are *Notiek* and a noun, with an ellipsis: *Notiek ielāde…*, *Notiek
  savienošana…*, *Notiek meklēšana…*. A request's status is a participle agreeing with
  *pieprasījums* (*Pieprasīts*, *Apstiprināts*, *Noraidīts*) or *Neizdevās*, also in titles and
  labels on the same screen.
- Sentence case, like the English. Latvian lowercases languages, days and months (*angļu*,
  *pirmdiena*, *janvāris*). Feature names keep their own capitals (*Watch Party*, *Autoscan*).
  An English all-caps label stays all caps (*TIEŠRAIDE* for LIVE).
- A library type or tab name used as a common noun inside a sentence is lowercase and declined
  (*nav filmu bibliotēku*). When a sentence names a screen, button or setting, put its label in
  „…”, unchanged, after a noun that takes the case: *sadaļā „Iestatījumi”*, *pogu „Saglabāt”*.
- Past-tense verbs have no gender, but participles and adjectives do, and the app knows nobody's
  gender. Use finite verbs for people (*Jūs pierakstījāties*, *{name} pievienojās*), not
  participles (*Jūs esat pierakstījies*), and never slash forms (*pierakstījies/-usies*). Role
  nouns take the masculine generic: *lietotājs*, *rīkotājs*, *viesis*, *skatītājs*.
- Keep every word that narrows what a sentence covers: *with {provider}*, *on this device*,
  *only*, *for this profile*. Dropping one changes what the sentence says.
- So a sign-in warning keeps its method (*Šajā kontā nevar pierakstīties, izmantojot
  {provider}*), and *only on this device* stays *tikai šajā ierīcē*.

## Placeholders and grammar

Latvian declines nouns, and the app inserts every placeholder value as it is, in the nominative.
You can't see the value, so you can't decline it.

- Never attach an ending to a placeholder: not `{server}ā`, `{user}a`, `{title}u`, `{count}-ās`.
- Put a common noun before the placeholder and decline that noun: *serverī {server}*,
  *Neizdevās atskaņot filmu „{title}”*, *Lietotāja {user} profils* (never *{user} profils*),
  *bibliotēkā „{library}”*. Carrier nouns: *serveris*, *bibliotēka*, *vienums* (an item),
  *lietotājs*, *profils*, *ierīce*, *fails*. Names people give to things (titles, libraries,
  collections, profiles, devices) go in „…”; a person's name, a host name, a number or a path
  stands bare.
- Or restructure so the placeholder stands alone: *{user} pievienojās*, *Serveris: {server}*.
- An adjective or participle agrees with the carrier noun, never with the placeholder: *Filma
  „{title}” ir izdzēsta*, *Vienums „{title}” ir izdzēsts*.
- Product, company, app and service names (*Silo*, *Plex*, *Apple TV*, *TMDB*) and Silo's
  feature names (*Watch Party*, *Autoscan*) are never declined and take no quotation marks, as
  Google writes them in Latvian. Put them before a noun that carries the case, or after a
  preposition: *Silo serveris*, *Apple TV ierīcē*, *pierakstīties ar Plex*, *iziet no Watch
  Party*. An undeclined name may also fill a case slot (*pievienoties Watch Party*); where that
  reads awkwardly, add a noun (*Silo lietotnē*). A word that must agree with *Watch Party* is
  masculine: *Šis Watch Party vairs nav pieejams*.
- A number placeholder keeps its noun after it, in the form the number requires (see Plurals).

## Typography

- Quotation marks „…”; a sentence's period or comma goes after the closing mark.
- Write the ellipsis as one character …, right after the word (*Notiek ielāde…*).
- Use the typographic apostrophe ’, never '. Latvian almost never needs one, and never to put an
  ending on a foreign name.
- A dash inside a sentence is an en dash with spaces ( – ); a range takes one without (*5–10*).
- An ordinal number in digits takes a period: *2. sezona*, *3. epizode*, *1. vieta*.
- Always write ā č ē ģ ī ķ ļ ņ š ū ž, and a comma before every subordinate clause (*ka*, *lai*,
  *kad*, *ja*, *kas*): *Nospiediet OK, lai turpinātu*.

## Vocabulary

- Standard Latvian UI terms as Google and Microsoft use them: *Iestatījumi*, *serveris*,
  *bibliotēka*, *subtitri*, *lejupielādēt*, *ierīce*, *konts*, *parole*, *lietotājs*,
  *multivide* (media), *atskaņošana* (playback), *atskaņot* (play), *pauzēt* (pause),
  *turpināt* (resume), *pēc noklusējuma* (by default), *kešatmiņa* (cache), *žurnāls* (log),
  *dublējums* (backup), *spraudnis* (plugin), *treileris*. Library types: *Filmas*, *Seriāli*,
  *Mūzika*, *Audiogrāmatas*. English stays only where it is code-like (*API*, *URL*, *webhook*).
- Standard forms, not the ones a model tends to borrow: *nākamais*, not *nākošais* (next);
  *iestatījumi*, not *uzstādījumi*; *pierakstīties* / *izrakstīties*, not *ielogoties*,
  *pieslēgties* / *atslēgties* (sign in / out); *pēc noklusējuma*, not *pēc noklusēšanas*; the
  dative or *paredzēts*, not *priekš* (*jums*, not *priekš jums*); *šis*, not *dotais*;
  *pašreizējais*, not *tekošais*.
- *tālrunis* for a phone, *planšetdators* for a tablet, *televizors* (*TV* where space is short)
  for the television as a device, *pults* for the remote.
- Click is *noklikšķināt*, tap *pieskarties*, press *nospiest*. On TV, prefer *Atlasiet* to
  *Nospiediet*; for a button on the remote, write *Nospiediet OK*. A direction is something the
  user presses: *Nospiediet bultiņu pa labi, lai atvērtu paneli*.

## Keep as written

Code, file paths, URLs, environment variable and setting names, text in backticks, hostnames,
units (*Mbps*, *kbps*, *GB*), resolutions (*1080p*), codec, protocol and format names (in their
usual capitals, *HTTP*, *HLS*), and keyboard keys (*Space*, *Enter*).

When a sentence names another screen, button, row, task or setting, use the label that element
has in the catalog in this language, not the English one. Write that label word for word, also
inside a menu path (*Settings → General → …*): don’t swap in a synonym or another wording of the
same idea. When the prompt lists the labels a string names, use the one on the string’s
platform. The glossary lists the names that recur.

## Plurals and numbers

- Plurals list exactly these CLDR categories, with the whole numbers each one takes:
  - `zero`: 0, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, …
  - `one`: 1, 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71, 81, …
  - `other`: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, …
  Write `#` where the number goes.
- After a number the noun keeps the case the sentence needs; only singular or plural changes,
  and adjectives, participles and verbs agree:
  - `zero` (0, 10–20, 30 … 100): plural, *Lejupielādētas # epizodes*, *# uzdevumi*;
  - `one` (1, 21, 31 … not 11): singular, *Lejupielādēta # epizode*, *# uzdevums*;
  - `other` (2–9, 22–29 … and fractions): plural, the same form as `zero`.
  - `{count, plural, zero {# epizodes} one {# epizode} other {# epizodes}}`; in another case,
    every form takes it (*# epizodē* / *# epizodēs*, *no # ierīces* / *no # ierīcēm*).
- `zero` is not "none": 10, 20 and 100 land there too, so it never says *nav* or *neviens*.
  `one` also covers 21 and 31, so it never spells out *viens* / *viena* or says *šis*.
- A number outside a plural can't change its noun: write *Epizodes: {count}*, *Skaits: {count}*.
- Where the English prints `{count_text}` instead of `#`, keep `{count_text}`: the app formats
  that number.
- Digits come from the app; don't write numbers out or change separators. An example of what to
  type into a field (*24h*, *30d, 2w, 6m*) is input syntax and stays exactly as written.
- A number and its unit are separated by a space: *24 h*, *2 h 4 min*, *45 s*, *211 ms*,
  *7 d.*, also after a number placeholder (*{minutes} min*). The percent sign follows the number
  without a space (*50%*, *{percent}%*). Never *m* for minutes. In a short title where a line
  break would strand the unit, use a no-break space (U+00A0).

## Length

Latvian runs 15–30% longer than English. Keys with `maxLength` appear on TV and small controls.
Stay within it: choose a shorter word (*Turpināt* for "Resume", *Atskaņot* for "Play", *TV* over
*televizors*), drop *jūsu* and the object before anything else, and use only the standard unit
abbreviations; never cut a word short.

## Season and episode codes

Every season and episode code uses the same letters across the catalog (codes, tags, captions,
play buttons, Watch Party lines, notifications, admin pages): *S{season}E{episode}*,
*S{season} · E{episode}*, *S{season}:E{episode}*, *E{episode}* (S for sezona, E for epizode).
Keep the English separators (none, · or :). Written out, a season and an episode are ordinals
with a period: *{season}. sezona*, *{episode}. epizode* (*2. sezona, 5. epizode*).
