# European Portuguese (pt-PT)

## Voice

- European Portuguese as written in Portugal, never Brazilian: vocabulary, grammar and spelling
  differ (see "European, not Brazilian" below). Spelling follows the 1990 Orthographic
  Agreement as used in Portugal: *ação*, *atual*, *coleção*, *receção*, but *facto*,
  *contacto*, *secção*, *género*, *eletrónico*.
- Address the user formally in the third person, with the subject left out, as Apple, Google
  and Netflix do in Portugal: *Inicie sessão*, *Escolha um perfil*, *Tente novamente*. Possessives
  take the article: *o seu perfil*, *a sua conta*. "You" after a preposition is *si* (*Para si*,
  *recomendado para si*). Never the *tu* forms (*Inicia sessão*, *o teu perfil*), never the
  pronoun *você*, which reads as curt in Portugal, and never *vocês*. Text that speaks to a group
  still addresses the reader, or uses the infinitive (*Ver juntos, estejam todos onde estiverem*).
- Buttons, menu items and the choices of a picker use the infinitive, consistently with the
  strings beside them: *Cancelar*, *Guardar*, *Eliminar*, *Tentar novamente*, *Iniciar sessão*,
  *Usar as suas preferências de legendas*. Instructions in sentences use the imperative:
  *Escolha uma biblioteca*. Labels of a setting, a toggle or a picker are noun phrases
  (*Reprodução automática*, *Tamanho das legendas*).
- An on/off value is *Ativado* / *Desativado*, masculine singular, whatever it switches.
- Progress states use *a* + infinitive with an ellipsis, never the gerund: *A carregar…*,
  *A ligar…*, *A transferir…* (not *Carregando…*). Running text does the same: *Quem está a
  ver?*, *O servidor está a transcodificar este ficheiro*.
- Agreement follows the noun the reader sees: a request's status agrees with *o pedido*
  (*Pedido*, *Aprovado*, *Recusado*, *Falhado*, *Concluído*), also in titles and labels on the
  same screen.
- Sentence case, like the English. Months, days and languages are lowercase (*janeiro*,
  *segunda-feira*, *inglês*). Feature names keep their own capitals (*Watch Party*,
  *Autoscan*). English all-caps labels stay all caps (*EM DIRETO* for LIVE). A library type or
  tab name used as a common noun inside a sentence is lowercase (*nenhuma biblioteca de filmes*).
- Be gender-neutral where it reads naturally: *Boas-vindas* rather than *Bem-vindo*, *quem
  organiza* for the host of a party. Never slashes, *@* or *x* (*convidado/a*, *convidad@s*).
- Keep every word that narrows what a sentence covers: *with {provider}*, *on this device*,
  *only*, *for this profile*. Dropping one changes what the sentence says.
- A warning that the user can't sign in *with* a method keeps it (*Deixará de poder iniciar
  sessão com {provider}*); without *com {provider}* it reads as a locked account.

## Placeholders and grammar

A placeholder holds a value you can't see: its gender and number are unknown.

- Portuguese fuses prepositions with the article (*do*, *da*, *no*, *na*, *ao*, *à*, *pelo*),
  and the article follows the gender of the value. Never put an article or a fused form straight
  before a placeholder (not *no {server}*, *da {library}*, *ao {user}*). Put a noun before it
  and let the article agree with that noun: *no servidor {server}*, *na biblioteca {library}*,
  *do perfil {name}*, *ao utilizador {user}*. A title can follow a bare *de* (*Episódios de
  {title}*), and a person placeholder stands without an article (*Convite de {name}*).
- Adjectives and past participles can't agree with a placeholder. Make them agree with a
  carrier noun (*O ficheiro {file} foi eliminado*), put them before a colon (*Adicionado:
  {title}*), or use a verb without a participle (*{title} já está na sua lista para ver*).
- Names in the glossary take an article like any noun, with the gender of what they are: *no
  Silo*, *do Plex*, *na Apple TV*, *na Android TV*, *no Roku*, *no Chromecast*.
- Pronouns follow the European order: after the verb, with a hyphen, in an affirmative main
  clause (*Junte-se à Watch Party*, *{user} ligou-se*, *O perfil foi-lhe atribuído*); before
  the verb after *não*, *que*, *já*, *só*, *também* and question words (*não se encontra
  disponível*, *o dispositivo que se ligou*). Never start a sentence with a pronoun (*Se
  junte*); for the future write *vai ligar-se*, not *ligar-se-á*.

## Typography

- Quotation marks «…», with no space inside; “…” for a quotation inside a quotation. Use them
  where the English quotes; don't add them around a label that the English leaves bare.
- No space before : ; ! ?. Write the ellipsis as one character …, never three dots. Use the
  typographic apostrophe ’, never '.
- Accents stay on capitals: *É necessário*, *Ação concluída*. Hyphens join a pronoun after its
  verb (*junte-se*, *atribua-lhe*) and compounds (*palavra-passe*, *pré-visualização*).

## Vocabulary

- The UI terms of Portugal, as Apple, Google and Netflix use them, with the table below:
  *servidor*, *biblioteca*, *legendas*, *dispositivo*, *conta*, *pasta*, *cópia de segurança*,
  *metadados*, *registo* (a log), *notificação*, *Início* (the Home screen).
- Upload is *carregar* (*Carregar imagem*); its progress state is *A enviar…*, so it doesn't
  read as *A carregar…* (Loading…).
- English words Portuguese keeps: *streaming*, *online*, *cache* (*a cache*), *plugin*,
  *trailer*, *link*, *email*, *token*, *webhook*, *app* (*a app*), *tablet*. *TV* (*a TV*) or
  *televisor* for the television as a device. Keep product and feature names from the glossary.
- On TV, prefer *Selecione* to *Prima*; for a button on the remote, write *Prima OK*. A remote
  button is the object of *Prima*, not the subject: *Prima Direita para abrir o painel*. On a
  touch screen write *Toque em*, with a mouse *Clique em*.

## Keep as written

Code, file paths, URLs, environment variable and setting names, text in backticks, hostnames,
units (*Mbps*, *kbps*, *GB*), resolutions (*1080p*), codec, protocol and format names (in their
usual capitals, *HTTP*, *HLS*), and keyboard keys (*Space*, *Enter*).

When a sentence names another screen, button, row, task or setting, use the label that element
has in the catalog in this language, not the English one. Write that label word for word, also
inside a menu path (*Settings → General → …*): don’t swap in a synonym or another wording of the
same idea. When the prompt lists the labels a string names, use the one on the string’s
platform. The glossary lists the names that recur.

## Plurals and numbers

- Plurals list exactly these CLDR categories, with the whole numbers each one takes:
  - `one`: 1
  - `many`: 1000000
  - `other`: 0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, …
  Write `#` where the number goes.
- `one` is only 1 (*1 episódio*); 0 takes `other` (*0 episódios*), unlike Brazilian Portuguese.
  `many` is for whole millions and puts *de* between the number and a noun that follows it
  directly (*1 000 000 de episódios*); with no noun right after the number (*Episódios: #*),
  `many` reads like `other`. Adjectives and participles agree with the noun (*1 episódio
  transferido*, *# temporadas novas*). Example:
  `{count, plural, one {# episódio} many {# de episódios} other {# episódios}}`.
- A number and its unit are separated by a space: *24 h*, *2 h 4 min*, *45 s*, *211 ms*,
  *7 d*, also after a number placeholder (*{minutes} min*). Never *m* for minutes. In a short
  title where a line break would strand the unit, use a no-break space (U+00A0).
- Where the English prints `{count_text}` instead of `#`, keep `{count_text}`: the app formats
  that number.
- Digits come from the app; don't write numbers out or change separators. An example of what to
  type into a field (*24h*, *30d, 2w, 6m*) is input syntax and stays exactly as written.

## Length

European Portuguese runs about a third longer than English. Keys with `maxLength` appear on TV
and small controls. Stay within it: choose a shorter word over an abbreviation (*Retomar* for
Resume), drop articles, possessives and the obvious complement before abbreviating
(*Definições de áudio*, not *As suas definições de áudio*), and never cut a word with a full
stop (*Def.*, *Transf.*).

## Season and episode codes

Every season and episode code uses the same letters across the catalog (codes, tags, captions,
play buttons, Watch Party lines, notifications, admin pages): *T{season}E{episode}*,
*T{season} · E{episode}*, *T{season}:E{episode}*, *E{episode}* (T for temporada, E for
episódio, as in Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese; never S). Keep the English separators (none,
· or :). Spelled-out text writes *Temporada {season}*, *Episódio {episode}*, and both together
as *Temporada {season}, episódio {episode}*.
