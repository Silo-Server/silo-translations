# Lithuanian (lt)

## Voice

- Address the user politely with the plural **jūs**, as Google and Microsoft do in Lithuanian:
  *Prisijunkite*, *Pasirinkite profilį*, *Bandykite dar kartą.*, *Jūsų biblioteka*. Write *jūs*,
  *jūsų* in lowercase and leave the pronoun out where the verb already says who. Never the
  informal *tu* (*Pasirink*, *tavo*), also in text that speaks to a group.
- Buttons, menu items and the choices of a picker use the infinitive, consistently with the
  strings beside them: *Išsaugoti*, *Atšaukti*, *Ištrinti*, *Prisijungti*, *Bandyti dar kartą*.
  Instructions in sentences use the *jūs* imperative: *Pasirinkite biblioteką*. Names of
  settings, screens and sections are nouns: *Atkūrimas*, *Atsisiuntimai*, *Srauto kokybė*.
- An on/off value is *Įjungta* / *Išjungta*, whatever it switches.
- Progress states use the neuter present passive participle with an ellipsis: *Įkeliama…*,
  *Jungiamasi…*, *Ieškoma…*. A request's status is a neuter past passive participle
  (*Užklausta*, *Patvirtinta*, *Atmesta*) or *Nepavyko*, also in titles and labels on the same
  screen.
- Sentence case, like the English. Lithuanian lowercases languages, days and months (*anglų*,
  *pirmadienis*, *sausis*). Feature names keep their own capitals (*Watch Party*, *Autoscan*).
  An English all-caps label stays all caps (*TIESIOGIAI* for LIVE).
- A library type or tab name used as a common noun inside a sentence is lowercase and declined
  (*nėra filmų bibliotekų*). When a sentence names a screen, button or setting, put its label in
  „…“, unchanged, after a noun that takes the case: *skiltyje „Nustatymai“*, *mygtuką
  „Išsaugoti“*.
- Past-tense verbs have no gender, but participles and adjectives do, and the app knows nobody's
  gender. Use finite verbs for people (*Prisijungėte*, *{name} prisijungė*), not participles
  (*Esate prisijungęs*), the neuter for results (*Išsaugota*, *Atsijungta*), and never slash
  forms (*prisijungęs (-usi)*). Role nouns take the masculine generic: *naudotojas*,
  *organizatorius*, *svečias*, *žiūrovas*.
- Keep every word that narrows what a sentence covers: *with {provider}*, *on this device*,
  *only*, *for this profile*. Dropping one changes what the sentence says.
- So a sign-in warning keeps its method (*Negalite prisijungti prie šios paskyros per
  „{provider}“*), and *only on this device* stays *tik šiame įrenginyje*.

## Placeholders and grammar

Lithuanian declines nouns in seven cases, and the app inserts every placeholder value as it is,
in the nominative. You can't see the value, so you can't decline it.

- Never attach an ending or an apostrophe to a placeholder: not `{server}yje`, `{user}o`,
  `{title}ą`, `{count}-ių`.
- Put a common noun before the placeholder and decline that noun: *serveryje {server}*,
  *Nepavyko paleisti filmo „{title}“*, *Naudotojo {user} profilis* (never *{user} profilis*),
  *bibliotekoje „{library}“*. Carrier nouns: *serveris*, *biblioteka*, *elementas* (an item),
  *naudotojas*, *profilis*, *įrenginys*, *failas*. Names people give to things (titles,
  libraries, collections, profiles, devices) go in „…“; a person's name, a host name, a number
  or a path stands bare.
- Or restructure so the placeholder stands alone: *{user} prisijungė*, *Serveris: {server}*.
- An adjective or participle agrees with the carrier noun, never with the placeholder: *Filmas
  „{title}“ ištrintas*, *Biblioteka „{library}“ ištrinta*.
- Product, company, app and service names (*Silo*, *Plex*, *Apple TV*, *TMDB*) and Silo's
  feature names (*Watch Party*, *Autoscan*) are never declined. Inside a sentence or before a
  noun, write them in „…“, as Google and Microsoft do in Lithuanian, and let a noun or a
  preposition carry the case: *„Silo“ serveris*, *„Apple TV“ įrenginyje*, *prisijungti per
  „Plex“*, *prisijungti prie „Watch Party“* (masculine where a word must agree: *Šis „Watch
  Party“ nebepasiekiamas*). A placeholder holding such a name does the same (*per
  „{provider}“*). A label that is only the name, and formats, codecs, protocols and
  abbreviations (*Dolby Vision*, *HDR10*, *HTTP*, *Wi-Fi*, *PIN*), take no quotes.
- A number placeholder keeps its noun after it, in the form the number requires (see Plurals).

## Typography

- Quotation marks „…“ (inner ‚…‘); a sentence's period or comma goes after the closing mark.
- Write the ellipsis as one character …, right after the word (*Įkeliama…*).
- Use the typographic apostrophe ’, never '. Lithuanian almost never needs one, and never to put
  an ending on a foreign name: *„Facebook“ paskyroje*, not *Facebook’e*.
- A dash inside a sentence is an en dash with spaces ( – ); a range takes one without (*5–10*).
- Always write ą č ę ė į š ų ū ž, and a comma before every subordinate clause (*kad*, *kai*,
  *jei*, *kuris*): *Paspauskite OK, kad tęstumėte*.

## Vocabulary

- Standard Lithuanian UI terms as Google and Microsoft use them: *Nustatymai*, *serveris*,
  *biblioteka*, *subtitrai*, *atsisiųsti*, *įrenginys*, *paskyra*, *slaptažodis*, *naudotojas*,
  *atkūrimas* (playback), *leisti* (play), *pristabdyti* (pause), *tęsti* (resume),
  *numatytasis* (default), *talpykla* (cache), *žurnalas* (log), *papildinys*, *anonsas*
  (trailer). Library types: *Filmai*, *Serialai*, *Muzika*, *Audioknygos*. English stays only
  where it is code-like (*API*, *URL*, *webhook*).
- Standard forms, not the ones a model tends to borrow: *kitas*, not *sekantis* (next);
  *atkurti*, not *atstatyti*; *naudotojas*, not *vartotojas*; *įkelti*, not *užkrauti*;
  *paleisti iš naujo*, not *perkrauti*; *numatytasis*, not *pagal nutylėjimą*; *visas ekranas*,
  not *pilnas ekranas*.
- *telefonas* for a phone, *planšetė* for a tablet, *televizorius* (*TV* where space is short)
  for the television as a device, *pultas* for the remote.
- Click is *spustelėti*, tap *paliesti*, press *paspausti*. On TV, prefer *Pasirinkite* to
  *Paspauskite*; for a button on the remote, write *Paspauskite OK*. A direction is something the
  user presses: *Paspauskite rodyklę dešinėn, kad atidarytumėte skydelį*.

## Keep as written

Code, file paths, URLs, environment variable and setting names, text in backticks, hostnames,
units (*Mbps*, *kbps*, *GB*), resolutions (*1080p*), codec, protocol and format names (in their
usual capitals, *HTTP*, *HLS*), and keyboard keys (*Space*, *Enter*).

When a sentence names another screen, button, row, task or setting, use the label that element
has in the catalog in this language, not the English one. Write that label word for word, also
inside a menu path (*Settings → General → …*): don’t swap in a synonym or another wording of the
same idea. When the prompt lists the labels a string names, use the one on the string’s
platform. The glossary lists the names that recur.

## Plurals and numbers

- Plurals list exactly these CLDR categories, with the whole numbers each one takes:
  - `one`: 1, 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71, 81, …
  - `few`: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, …
  - `many`: only fractions (never a whole number)
  - `other`: 0, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, …
  Write `#` where the number goes.
- Each category has its own noun form, and adjectives, participles and verbs agree with it:
  - `one` (1, 21, 31 … not 11): nominative singular, *Ištrintas # epizodas*, *# užduotis*;
  - `few` (2–9, 22–29 … not 12–19): nominative plural, *Ištrinti # epizodai*, *# užduotys*;
  - `many` (fractions only): genitive singular, *# epizodo*, *# valandos*; whole counts never
    reach it, but every plural carries it;
  - `other` (0, 10–20, 30 … 100): genitive plural with a neuter verb, *Ištrinta # epizodų*,
    *# užduočių*.
  - `{count, plural, one {# epizodas} few {# epizodai} many {# epizodo} other {# epizodų}}`.
- `one` also covers 21 and 31, so it never spells out *vienas* / *viena* or says *šis*. A number
  outside a plural can't decline its noun: write *Epizodų: {count}*, *Liko: {count}*.
- Where the English prints `{count_text}` instead of `#`, keep `{count_text}`: the app formats
  that number.
- Digits come from the app; don't write numbers out or change separators. An example of what to
  type into a field (*24h*, *30d, 2w, 6m*) is input syntax and stays exactly as written.
- A number and its unit are separated by a space, with the Lithuanian abbreviations: *24 val.*,
  *2 val. 4 min.*, *45 sek.*, *211 ms*, *7 d.*, *50 %*, also after a number placeholder
  (*{minutes} min.*, *{percent} %*). Never *m* for minutes. In a short title where a line break
  would strand the unit, use a no-break space (U+00A0).

## Length

Lithuanian runs 20–30% longer than English, and its words are long. Keys with `maxLength` appear
on TV and small controls. Stay within it: choose a shorter word (*Tęsti* for "Resume", *Leisti*
for "Play", *TV* over *televizorius*), drop *jūsų* and the object before anything else, and use
only the standard abbreviations (*val.*, *min.*, *sek.*); never cut a word short.

## Season and episode codes

Every season and episode code uses the same letters across the catalog (codes, tags, captions,
play buttons, Watch Party lines, notifications, admin pages): *S{season}E{episode}*,
*S{season} · E{episode}*, *S{season}:E{episode}*, *E{episode}* (S for sezonas, E for epizodas).
Keep the English separators (none, · or :). Written out, the number comes before a singular noun
and reads as an ordinal: *{season} sezonas*, *{episode} epizodas* (*2 sezonas, 5 epizodas*).
