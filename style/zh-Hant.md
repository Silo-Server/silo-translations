# Chinese, Traditional (zh-Hant)

Write Traditional Chinese as it is used in Taiwan. This is a translation in Taiwan usage, not a
character conversion of the Simplified text: many Mainland words become wrong or odd in Taiwan
even when written in Traditional characters (*視頻*, *軟件*, *設置*, *用戶*, *默認*). Follow
Apple's, Google's and Netflix's Taiwan (zh-TW) localizations. Write every character in its
Traditional form (*說*, *設定*, *裡*, *為*, *檔*), never a Simplified one.

## Voice

- Address the user as **你**, never 您. Friendly and direct: *登入*、*選擇個人檔案*、*請再試一次*。
- Buttons, menu items and the choices of a picker are short verb phrases, consistently with the
  strings beside them: *取消*、*儲存*、*重試*、*使用你的字幕偏好設定*。 Instructions in
  sentences use the plain imperative: *選擇媒體庫*。
- An on/off value is *開啟* / *關閉*, whatever it switches.
- Status words on one screen use one form: a request's status is a short phrase (*已請求*、
  *已核准*、*已拒絕*、*失敗*、*已完成*), also in titles and labels on the same screen.
- Feature names keep their glossary form (*Watch Party*、*Autoscan*). English all-caps labels
  become the normal Chinese word (*直播*).
- Don't put tab names in quotes when they are used as ordinary nouns in a sentence.
- Be gender-neutral: name the role (*使用者*、*對方*、*主持人*、*參與者*) or omit the pronoun, as
  Chinese allows; never 他 or 她 for an unknown person.

## Typography

- Full-width punctuation ，。、：；！？（） in Chinese text; no half-width commas or periods.
- Quotation marks are 「」, and 『』 for a quotation inside a quotation. Never “” or "".
- The ellipsis is …… (two characters) after Chinese text. Where the English has a single …
  after a verb on a button or menu item (*Save as…*), write …… too.
- Put a half-width space between Chinese and Latin words or digits, as Apple's and Google's
  Taiwan localizations do: *在 Apple TV 上*、*5 個項目*、*Watch Party 邀請*。 No space between
  a Latin word or digit and full-width punctuation (*Silo。*). Placeholders count as Latin text:
  *{count} 個*、*已連線至 {server}*。
- Keep placeholders exactly as they are.

## Keep as written

Code, file paths, URLs, environment variable and setting names, text in backticks, hostnames,
units (*Mbps*, *kbps*, *GB*), resolutions (*1080p*), codec, protocol and format names (in their
usual capitals, *HTTP*, *HLS*), and keyboard keys (*Space*, *Enter*).

When a sentence names another screen, button, row, task or setting, use the Chinese label that
element has in the catalog, not the English one. Write that label word for word, also inside a menu
path (*Settings → General → …*): don’t swap in a synonym or another wording of the same idea.
When the prompt lists the labels a string names, use the one on the string’s platform. The
glossary lists the names that recur.

## Plurals and numbers

- Plurals: only `other`; use measure words (*# 季*, *# 個項目*, *# 集*).
- Where the English prints `{count_text}` instead of `#`, keep `{count_text}`.
- Digits come from the app; don't write numbers out or change separators.
- Time units are Chinese words after the number with a space: *24 小時*, *2 小時 4 分鐘*,
  *45 秒*, *211 毫秒*, *7 天*, also after a number placeholder (*{minutes} 分鐘*). Latin units
  take a space too (*20 Mbps*). An example of what to type into a field (*24h*, *30d, 2w, 6m*)
  is input syntax and stays exactly as written.

## Length

Keys with `maxLength` appear on TV and small controls. Chinese is usually short; stay within the
limit by choosing a shorter phrase, and drop the spaces around Latin text only as a last resort.

## Season and episode codes

Every season and episode code uses the same letters across the catalog (codes, tags, captions,
play buttons, Watch Party lines, notifications, admin pages): *S{season}E{episode}* in a compact
code (keys named …Code, …Tag, …Fallback); a caption or line may write
*第 {season} 季 · 第 {episode} 集*. Keep the English separators (none, · or :).
